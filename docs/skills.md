# The 12 skills

Each skill below lists the situation it's built for, what goes wrong without it, and what you get. The `folder` is the skill's name once installed in Claude.

## Scope pack

*“Don't tell us what we already know.”*

### Question Zero

Maps what your team already knows, and how it knows it, then turns the gaps into ranked research questions.

- **The situation:** A new study is about to start, and the team says it already knows a lot.
- **The problem:** Nobody has written down what the team knows, or how it knows it.
- **What it costs you:** The study rediscovers old findings, and “don't tell us what we already know” lands on you at the readout.
- **What you get:** You start from the team's knowledge, test only what's unproven, and show at the end exactly what the research added.
- **What it needs from you:** A product area and a research question. Journey stages, attendees and any past evidence help.
- **Folder:** `question-zero-uxr`

### Diagnostic Partner

Works through a solution-shaped brief and funnel data with the PM, and turns it into a research-ready problem.

- **The situation:** A PM brings a brief that already names the answer: “test a shorter form.”
- **The problem:** The brief is a solution, so the study would only test that one idea.
- **What it costs you:** Weeks go into research that confirms or kills one idea while the real cause of the drop-off goes unexamined.
- **What you get:** You and the PM find where the problem sits in the funnel, rule out causes with data, and scope research only for what's left.
- **What it needs from you:** The brief as the PM wrote it, and any funnel or behaviour data.
- **Folder:** `diagnostic-partner`

### Research Plan Creator

Turns kickoff calls, briefs and ad hoc asks into a stakeholder-ready research plan.

- **The situation:** You have a kickoff transcript, a brief and three new asks in chat.
- **The problem:** Turning that into a plan stakeholders sign off takes days, and the plan changes mid-study.
- **What it costs you:** Scope creeps, the plan drifts, and nobody can point to what was agreed.
- **What you get:** A stakeholder-ready plan from the raw notes, versioned every time scope or stakeholders change.
- **What it needs from you:** A kickoff transcript, brief or a few lines on the decision the research serves.
- **Folder:** `research-plan-creator`

## Transcript Collator pack

*“Fieldwork's done. Now a week of retyping.”*

### Transcript Collator (free until 11 December 2026)

Turns raw transcripts into one theme-tagged notesheet, with a verbatim quote and timestamp on every row.

- **The situation:** Fieldwork is done: ten interviews, ten long transcripts.
- **The problem:** Getting them into one structured notesheet takes most of a day per session.
- **What it costs you:** Synthesis starts late, the second half of long sessions gets skimmed, and quotes get tidied or invented under deadline.
- **What you get:** One notesheet mapped to your themes, with a verbatim quote and timestamp on every row, ready for synthesis the same day.
- **What it needs from you:** Interview transcripts. A plan, discussion guide or existing notesheet makes the headers sharper.
- **Folder:** `uxr-transcript-collator`

### Collated Synth

Reads each participant's notes start to finish for beliefs, contradictions and tensions, as cited leads you judge.

- **The situation:** Your notesheet is ready and sorted by theme.
- **The problem:** Themes show what people said. They hide what people believe and where they contradict themselves.
- **What it costs you:** The insight that explains the behaviour never reaches the deck.
- **What you get:** Cited leads on beliefs, contradictions and tensions, each with evidence for and against, for you to judge.
- **What it needs from you:** Collated rows (from the Transcript Collator or the same 11-column format) and the study objective.
- **Folder:** `uxr-collated-synth`

### Collation Sorter

Sorts the collated sheet by theme and by participant in two new tabs, leaving the original untouched.

- **The situation:** Your collation sheet has hundreds of rows in transcript order.
- **The problem:** Reading it theme by theme, or person by person, means sorting by hand.
- **What it costs you:** Manual sorting breaks the original order and risks losing rows.
- **What you get:** Two new tabs, one by theme and one by participant, with the original left as it was.
- **What it needs from you:** A collation workbook with a Collation tab and a tab listing your theme order.
- **Folder:** `run-uxr-collation-sheet-sort-by-theme`

## Analysis pack

*“Fifty crosstabs and no story.”*

### Quant Analyst

Runs survey, usability and behaviour data against your research objectives instead of every possible crosstab.

- **The situation:** A survey export lands with 40 questions and six segments.
- **The problem:** Every crosstab is possible, so every crosstab gets run.
- **What it costs you:** The deck fills with numbers nobody asked for, and the answer to the research question gets lost.
- **What you get:** Analysis locked to your plan's objectives, with stakeholders agreeing the cuts before any number is run.
- **What it needs from you:** A survey, usability or log data file (Excel or CSV) and the 2 to 4 questions it was collected to answer.
- **Folder:** `uxr-quant-analyst`

### Report Builder

Builds a report, deck or topline from your real research data, ready for stakeholder review.

- **The situation:** Findings are ready and the readout is on Thursday.
- **The problem:** Turning a notesheet or survey into a report people read takes longer than the analysis did.
- **What it costs you:** Leadership skims, stops at slide five, and the finding never reaches the decision.
- **What you get:** A report that leads with the answer, in the format your audience reads, with every claim traceable to data.
- **What it needs from you:** Real data: a notesheet, survey results, transcripts, or an existing report to restructure.
- **Folder:** `uxr-report-builder`

## Influence pack

*“Great findings. Nothing changed.”*

### Research-Driven Prototype

Turns findings into an HTML prototype where every design change links back to a cited insight.

- **The situation:** The research says what's wrong with the product.
- **The problem:** Stakeholders can't picture what that means on screen.
- **What it costs you:** Designers fill the gap with guesses, and the research gets credit for none of it.
- **What you get:** A clickable prototype where every change sits next to the quote that justifies it.
- **What it needs from you:** The findings report, the notesheet and the discussion guide.
- **Folder:** `research-driven-prototype`

### Stakeholder Panel

Reviews any research artefact through six stakeholder voices, from the CEO to the data scientist.

- **The situation:** Your plan, guide or deck is about to go to real stakeholders.
- **The problem:** You can't hear their objections until the meeting.
- **What it costs you:** The meeting turns into a defence, and the work gets sent back.
- **What you get:** Six stakeholder voices raise their objections first, so you fix the gaps before you present.
- **What it needs from you:** Any research artefact: a plan, guide, screener, analysis sheet, report or deck.
- **Folder:** `stakeholder-panel`

### Stop AI Slop

A final editing pass that strips AI-writing tells from anything you're about to send.

- **The situation:** You drafted a report, email or post with AI.
- **The problem:** It reads like AI: filler openers, “not X but Y” lines, em dashes.
- **What it costs you:** Readers notice, trust drops, and the finding gets dismissed along with the prose.
- **What you get:** Text that reads like you wrote it, with quotes and numbers untouched.
- **What it needs from you:** Any draft text. Output skills in the stack also call it automatically.
- **Folder:** `stop-ai-slop`

## Career pack

*“The work was strong. The portfolio isn't.”*

### Case Study Builder

Turns project documents into portfolio case studies built the way hiring managers read them.

- **The situation:** You have finished projects and an interview coming up.
- **The problem:** Your case studies describe methods. Hiring managers look for framing and impact.
- **What it costs you:** Strong work reads as average, and the panel moves on.
- **What you get:** Case studies built the way hiring managers read them, tuned to the team you're meeting.
- **What it needs from you:** The role or team you're targeting, and whatever project documents you have.
- **Folder:** `uxr-case-study-builder`
