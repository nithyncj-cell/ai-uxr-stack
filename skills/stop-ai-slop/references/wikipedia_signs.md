# Formatting and register tells

This list draws on the Wikipedia project page "Signs of AI writing" (WikiProject AI Cleanup). It is a summary in this skill's own words, tuned for research writing. For the full and current list, read the page itself.

Use this file for patterns at the level of formatting, register and paragraph shape. `tells.md` covers phrases and sentence structures.

---

## Punctuation and formatting

**Em-dash overuse.** Machine prose leans on em dashes to splice clauses. Replace with a comma, colon, parentheses or a full stop. Hyphen ranges ("12-16") are not a fix; write "12 to 16" or "12 of 16".

**Bold lead-in bullets.** Every bullet starts with a bolded label and a colon ("**Speed:** the app is fast"). Turn it into a sentence unless the content is a real list of parallel items.

**Over-bolding.** Bold on every key term flattens emphasis. Keep bold for one thing per section at most, or none.

**Emoji in headers.** Remove from headings and slide titles in reports, plans and emails.

**Title Case Headings Everywhere.** Use sentence case unless the house style says otherwise.

**Lists where prose fits.** A two-sentence point split into four bullets. Write the two sentences.

## Register

**Inflated significance.** "plays a pivotal role", "serves as a testament to", "marks a turning point", "underscores the importance of", "a rich tapestry". State what happened and what it changes for whom.

**Promotional tone.** "seamless", "cutting-edge", "robust", "groundbreaking", "world-class". Findings are not marketing.

**Vague attribution.** "Studies show", "experts agree", "research suggests", "many users feel". Name the source and the base ("7 of 12 participants", "the 2024 retention survey, n=410"), or delete the claim.

**Tacked-on -ing analysis.** A clause added to the end of a fact to gesture at meaning: "...highlighting the need for better onboarding", "...reflecting a broader shift in user expectations". Delete it, or turn it into its own sentence with evidence.

**Negation-then-assertion.** "not X, but Y" and its variants. Covered in `tells.md`; the overlap is deliberate because it's the strongest single signal.

**Rule-of-three reflex.** Three adjectives, three examples, three parallel clauses, over and over. Vary the count.

## Paragraph and section shape

**Section-summary filler.** "In summary", "Overall", "In conclusion", "To sum up" restating a short passage. Cut it. A summary earns its place only after a long section.

**Formulaic challenges-and-future paragraphs.** "Despite these challenges, the future looks promising..." Delete, or replace with a specific next step and owner.

**Balanced-on-purpose hedging.** Every claim followed by "however, it's important to consider...". Put real limitations in the limitations section; delete the reflex ones.

**Uniform paragraph length.** Every paragraph three sentences long. Vary length to match the weight of the point.

## Chat leftovers

Text that only makes sense in a chat window and must never reach a deliverable:

- "Certainly!", "Great question!", "I hope this helps.", "Let me know if you'd like me to..."
- "As an AI...", knowledge-cutoff disclaimers
- Placeholder brackets left unfilled: "[Insert stakeholder name]"
- Notes to the user inside the document: "(You may want to add a chart here.)"

Remove all of these before the text is handed back.
